Sources

  1. Coffee Board of India. White stem borer, bio-ecology (two emergence peaks, April–May and October–December) and cultural management (remove and burn infested stems before mid-September; keep shade at the right level during flight periods).
  2. Coffee berry borer: the Coffee Board advises picking on time and picking clean, catching fallen berries on mats, clearing off-season and fallen berries, drying coffee to the right moisture, setting Broca traps and spraying the fungus Beauveria bassiana when the borer first appears (Coffee Board, Planter page). Its estate calendar schedules the clearing of infested fallen berries for March and April, after the harvest (Indian Coffee, March–April 2020).
  3. FAO. Coffee pests: mealybugs are generally more of a problem in the dry season, and heavy outbreaks often follow insecticide sprays that kill their natural enemies.
  4. Caterpillars on shade trees, the natural enemies, frogs and coral trees: our pages What Happens in a Pest Outbreak and Who Eats Whom, and the references listed there.
